### utah.w1 · The Narrows, Bottom Up
There is no trail. You walk up the Virgin River between walls a thousand feet high and twenty feet apart. Check the flash flood forecast first, every time.

### utah.w2 · Emerald Pools
Three tiers of pools fed by seeps in the sandstone, with a waterfall you walk behind at the lower one.

### utah.w3 · Lower Calf Creek Falls
A 126 ft fall dropping into a green pool in the middle of the desert, with pictographs on the wall a mile in.

### utah.w4 · Kanarra Falls
Up a creek into a narrow slot with wooden ladders bolted beside the falls. Permit capped at 150 people a day.

### utah.w5 · Mill Creek Falls
A swimming hole with a waterfall five minutes from downtown Moab. The whole town is there on a hot afternoon.

### utah.s1 · Delicate Arch
Up an open slickrock ramp with no shade and no railing, to the arch on the licence plate. Sunset is worth the crowd.

### utah.s2 · Landscape Arch
306 ft across and six feet thick at its thinnest. A slab fell off it in 1991 and nobody is allowed underneath any more.

### utah.s3 · Mesa Arch
An arch on the cliff edge that catches reflected sunrise on its underside. Expect forty tripods at dawn.

### utah.s4 · Navajo Loop & Queens Garden
Down Wall Street's switchbacks into the hoodoos and back up through the Queens Garden. The classic Bryce walk, done in the right direction.

### utah.s5 · Corona Arch
On BLM land outside the park, with a cable handrail and a short ladder. Bigger than most arches inside Arches.

### utah.v1 · Dead Horse Point
2,000 ft straight down onto a gooseneck of the Colorado, from a mesa connected to the world by a thirty yard neck.

### utah.v2 · Bryce Point at Sunrise
The whole amphitheatre lit from the east at once. It is 8,300 ft here and freezing at dawn most of the year.

### utah.v3 · Angels Landing
Walter's Wiggles, then half a mile of fin with chains bolted to the rock and 1,200 ft of air on both sides. Permit by lottery.

### utah.v4 · Muley Point & the Moki Dugway
Three miles of unpaved switchbacks up an 1,100 ft cliff, then a rim looking down on the San Juan goosenecks and out to Monument Valley.

### utah.m1 · Chesler Park & the Joint Trail
A meadow ringed by banded sandstone spires, reached through a fracture so narrow you turn your pack sideways.

### utah.m2 · Cassidy Arch
One of the few arches you are allowed to walk out on top of. Named for Butch Cassidy, who is supposed to have hidden below it.

### utah.m3 · Fiery Furnace
A maze of fins with no marked trail, entered by permit only after a briefing. People get lost in here every season.

### utah.m4 · Peekaboo & Spooky Gulch
Two slots off Hole-in-the-Rock Road, one entered by a ten foot climb and the other narrow enough that packs come off.

### utah.m5 · Hickman Bridge & Rim Overlook
A 133 ft natural bridge, then a climb to the rim looking down on the orchards and the Fremont River.

### utah.h1 · Observation Point
700 ft higher than Angels Landing and looking down on it, reached from East Mesa since the main trail closed to rockfall.

### utah.h2 · Mount Peale, La Sals
12,721 ft of loose talus with no trail above the basin, and the desert 8,000 ft below on every side.

### utah.h3 · Druid Arch
Up Elephant Canyon on sand and slickrock to a squared-off arch that looks like it was set there on purpose. Ladder near the top.

### utah.h4 · Mount Ellen, Henry Mountains
11,522 ft on the last range in the lower 48 to be mapped, with a free-roaming bison herd on the flanks below.

### utah.h5 · Boulder Mail Trail
The route the mail went by mule until 1924, across bare slickrock between Escalante and Boulder. Cairns only, and two deep canyon crossings.

### utah.l1 · Devils Garden Primitive Loop
Eight arches on one loop, with a slickrock fin you walk along and a section that is genuinely exposed.

### utah.l2 · Syncline Loop
Around Upheaval Dome, a crater that is either a salt dome or a meteor strike depending who you ask. More rescues than any other trail in the park.

### utah.l3 · West Rim Trail
Lava Point down to the Grotto, along the rim the whole way and past the Angels Landing junction at the end. One way, arrange a shuttle.

### utah.l4 · Under-the-Rim: Bryce Point to Sheep Creek
Below the hoodoos rather than above them, through ponderosa and past the Hat Shop. Almost nobody leaves the rim.

### utah.x1 · The Zion Traverse
Kolob Canyons to the East Rim across the whole park, over the plateau and down through the canyon. Water sources are far apart and you plan around them.

### utah.x2 · Coyote Gulch
A creek running through a 400 ft slot with two arches and a natural bridge over it. You walk in the water most of the way.

### utah.x3 · The Maze
The most remote district in the national park system. Three hours of high clearance road to the trailhead and no help coming if it goes wrong.

### utah.x4 · Grand Gulch, Cedar Mesa
Cliff dwellings, granaries and rock art left where they were built eight hundred years ago. Look, photograph, touch nothing.

### utah.x5 · Buckskin Gulch & Paria Canyon
The longest slot canyon on earth: thirteen miles of it under 500 ft walls twelve feet apart, with no exit and no high ground.

### utah.x6 · Under-the-Rim Trail, End to End
Bryce Point to Rainbow Point below the amphitheatre, camping among hoodoos at 8,000 ft where it freezes in June.

### utah.x7 · La Sal Traverse
Above 11,000 ft along an alpine range that has red desert on both horizons. Afternoon storms arrive without warning up here.
