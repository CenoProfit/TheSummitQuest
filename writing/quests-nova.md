### nova.w1 · Great Falls Overlooks
The Potomac drops 76 feet through Mather Gorge in a series of jagged ledges. Three railed overlooks, and the roar carries to the parking lot. No swimming — this stretch kills people every year.

### nova.w2 · Whiteoak Canyon Falls
Six waterfalls stacked down one hemlock gorge, the tallest 86 feet. Deep green pools between each drop. The bottom-up approach from Berry Hollow is the shorter climb.

### nova.w3 · Cedar Run Falls
A natural waterslide into a chest-deep chute, plus a 34-foot falls above it. Rockier and steeper than Whiteoak next door, and usually quieter.

### nova.w4 · Dark Hollow Falls
The closest waterfall to Skyline Drive and the busiest in the park. Seventy feet of cascade over greenstone. Steep on the way back out.

### nova.w5 · Rose River Falls
A 67-foot double drop on a loop that also passes an old copper mine and a stretch of Skyline's best hemlock. Far fewer people than Dark Hollow.

### nova.w6 · Lewis Spring Falls
An 81-foot falls over columnar basalt, reached from Big Meadows. Pair it with the Blackrock viewpoint on the return for the best half-day in the central district.

### nova.w7 · Overall Run Falls
The tallest waterfall in the park at 93 feet, with a rock ledge that looks straight down it. Runs thin by August — go in spring or after rain.

### nova.w8 · South River Falls
Third-tallest in Shenandoah at 83 feet. The observation platform is good, but the spur to the base is where the swimming is.

### nova.w9 · Jones Run & Doyles River Falls
Four waterfalls on one loop in the quiet south district. Wading pools at the base of the 42-foot Jones Run drop, and almost nobody on the trail midweek.

### nova.w10 · Scott's Run Falls
A creek drops over a rock shelf straight into the Potomac. Twenty-five minutes from Tysons and it feels like the mountains. Swimming is banned and enforced.

### nova.w11 · Passage Creek, Elizabeth Furnace
Clear shallow pools along Fort Valley's creek, with flat rock to lie on and an 1836 iron furnace beside the picnic area. The easiest cold-water afternoon in the region.

### nova.w12 · Crabtree Falls
Five major cascades totalling 1,200 feet, the tallest series east of the Mississippi. Paved to the first overlook, then stairs and switchbacks. People have died stepping onto the wet rock.

### nova.w13 · Shenandoah Confluence, Harpers Ferry
Where the Shenandoah meets the Potomac between two states. Wade the rocks off Virginius Island, or put in with an outfitter and float the whole confluence.

### nova.s1 · Bearfence Mountain Scramble
A short hands-on rock scramble to a genuine 360-degree view — the only unobstructed full circle in the park. Not for anyone uneasy with exposure.

### nova.s2 · Compton Peak Columnar Basalt
Two spurs from one summit: north for the view, south for a wall of hexagonal basalt columns left by a lava flow 570 million years ago. Take the south one.

### nova.s3 · Little Stony Man
The best view-per-step ratio on Skyline Drive. Nine tenths of a mile to open cliffs above the Shenandoah Valley. Elite sunset with almost no commitment.

### nova.s4 · Stony Man Summit
Second-highest peak in the park at 4,011 feet on a gentle graded trail. Peregrine falcons work the cliffs below the summit rocks.

### nova.s5 · Blackrock Summit
A flat mile ends at a jumbled field of quartzite talus you scramble over for a wide south-district view. Absurdly good for the effort.

### nova.s6 · Turk Mountain
Short descent then a climb to a talus summit facing west over the Shenandoah Valley. One of the quietest views in the park.

### nova.s7 · Sugarloaf Mountain
An isolated Maryland monadnock standing 800 feet above flat farmland. Privately owned, free to enter, quartzite outcrops all over the summit ridge.

### nova.s8 · Bears Den Overlook
A quarter mile from the parking area to a west-facing rock pile on the Appalachian Trail. The classic Loudoun sunset, and the hostel behind it sells ice cream.

### nova.v1 · Hogback Overlook
The widest pullout view on Skyline Drive, looking down on seven bends of the Shenandoah River. Best in the last hour of light.

### nova.v2 · Range View Overlook
Straight down the spine of the Blue Ridge with Hogback, Marshall and Compton stacked in a row. The name is exactly accurate.

### nova.v3 · Maryland Heights
The photograph of Harpers Ferry — the town on its wedge of rock with two rivers closing around it. Steep climb, Civil War stone fortifications on the way up.

### nova.v4 · Raven Rocks
Four hard climbs on the Appalachian Trail section thru-hikers call the Roller Coaster, ending on cliffs above the Shenandoah Valley. Deceptively tough for an hour from home.

### nova.v5 · Buzzard Rock
A narrow rock fin on Massanutten's north end with drops on both sides and vultures riding the thermals at eye level. Exposed ridge walking most of the way.

### nova.v6 · Big Schloss
A sandstone castle on the Virginia-West Virginia line, reached by a wooden footbridge across a gap in the rock. Schloss is German for castle and the settlers were not exaggerating.

### nova.v7 · Piedmont Overlook, Sky Meadows
A steep pasture climb to a bench looking east over the whole Piedmont. Stone walls, grazing cattle and hang gliders off Ashby Gap. Best picnic hill in Virginia.

### nova.m1 · Mary's Rock via Meadow Spring
The short brutal way up to a granite outcrop above Thornton Gap, with a 360 that takes in Luray, the valley and the Blue Ridge running north. Half the distance of the Panorama route.

### nova.m2 · Hawksbill Summit Loop
The highest point in Shenandoah at 4,051 feet, with a stone observation platform and balsam fir that has no business this far south. Loop it up the Lower Hawksbill and down the AT.

### nova.m3 · Bull Run-Occoquan Trail
Follows the creek through bottomland forest. In the second week of April the floodplain turns solid blue with the largest Virginia bluebell bloom in the state.

### nova.m4 · Prince William Forest South Valley
Fifteen thousand acres of second-growth piedmont forest twenty minutes off I-95. Abandoned pyrite mine workings and CCC cabin camps hidden in the woods.

### nova.m5 · Manassas Battlefield Double Loop
Both battles walked in one go: Henry Hill, the Stone Bridge, Chinn Ridge and Deep Cut. Open field walking with interpretive markers and the Stone House still standing.

### nova.m6 · Cunningham Falls & Hog Rock
Maryland's tallest cascading falls at 78 feet, looped with a rock overlook above the Monocacy valley. Catoctin's other claim to fame is Camp David, which you will not be visiting.

### nova.m7 · High Knob & Elizabeth Furnace Loop
A steady climb out of Fort Valley to a ridge view, then down past the furnace ruins. Good early-season legs-builder before the Massanutten scrambles.

### nova.m8 · Billy Goat Trail Section A
A blue-blazed boulder scramble along the Maryland lip of Mather Gorge, including the Traverse — a slanted rock face with the river a long way below. Single file and busy.

### nova.h1 · Old Rag Mountain
The most famous hike in Virginia and the most technical: a half mile of granite chute scrambling through boulder tunnels and up chimneys. Ticketed entry March to November, and it still fills.

### nova.h2 · Strickler Knob
A spine of tilted quartzite you scramble along to a pinnacle over Fort Valley. The final half mile is unmarked and easy to lose — follow the rock, not the ground.

### nova.h3 · Duncan Knob
Ends with a straight hands-and-feet haul up a talus cone to a boulder summit. Short, steep and genuinely fun. Sisters to Strickler across the hollow.

### nova.h4 · Robertson Mountain
The steepest sustained climb in the central district and almost nobody on it, because everyone next door is queuing for Old Rag. Limited view, serious legs.

### nova.h5 · Three Ridges
A 3,000-foot Appalachian Trail classic over Chimney Rock and Hanging Rock with the Priest staring back across the gap. One of the two hardest day loops in the state.

### nova.h6 · The Priest
Three thousand feet of climbing in four miles with no relief. Thru-hikers write confessions in the shelter register at the top. The view is worth the penance.

### nova.h7 · Mount Marshall via Bluff Trail
A long circuit on the quiet north-district side slopes, crossing several streams before the climb. Bear country — this stretch has the highest density in the park.

### nova.l1 · Whiteoak & Cedar Run Loop
The Shenandoah waterfall grand slam: eight named drops down one gorge and up the next, with swimming holes the whole way. The single best day hike in the park.

### nova.l2 · Old Rag, Robertson & Corbin Hollow
Links the famous scramble with the mountain nobody climbs and an old mountaineer hollow between them. A full day with 4,000 feet of gain.

### nova.l3 · Signal Knob Loop
Massanutten's north end, where Confederate signalmen watched the whole valley. Rocky ridge walking with views both east and west. Carry all your water — there is none up there.

### nova.l4 · Riprap & Wildcat Ridge Loop
Ridge views, a rock outcrop over Chimney Rock hollow, and a genuine swimming hole in Riprap Run at the low point. Best loop in the south district.

### nova.l5 · Big Run Loop
The largest watershed in the park, dropping 2,000 feet into a wide wild basin and climbing back out on Rockytop. Trout water at the bottom and hardly anyone anywhere.

### nova.l6 · Rocky Mount & Gap Run
Down before up, which means the hard climbing is at the end. Burn-scarred ridge with open views and no water source. Wilderness — no blazes past the first mile.

### nova.l7 · Tuscarora: Elizabeth Furnace Circuit
Stitches the Tuscarora and Massanutten trails around Fort Valley's north end over both flanking ridges. Long, rocky and rarely walked in one push.

### nova.x1 · Shenandoah AT Thru-Hike
The whole park end to end on the Appalachian Trail, Rockfish Gap to Front Royal. Waysides and huts spaced for resupply, which makes it the friendliest long thru-hike in the mid-Atlantic.

### nova.x2 · Massanutten Trail Loop
A complete circuit of the mountain that sits inside the Shenandoah Valley, ringing Fort Valley on both ridges. Relentlessly rocky and famously hard on feet. Water is the whole problem.

### nova.x3 · Tuscarora Trail: North Section
The old alternate route to the AT, running the western ridges through Virginia into West Virginia. Poorly travelled, sparsely marked, and completely empty most weekends.

### nova.x4 · Potomac Heritage Section
Follows the Virginia bank from Theodore Roosevelt Island up past Great Falls, with the river always to your right. The only overnight here that starts inside the Beltway.

### nova.x5 · Old Rag & Central District Backpack
The scramble, the waterfall gorges and the high AT ridge linked into one overnight with a backcountry permit. The best two nights in the park, and the hardest to get a site for.

### nova.x6 · Big Run & Riprap Overnight
The gentlest way into backpacking here: established sites, reliable trout streams, a swimming hole at Riprap, and a south district that stays empty even on holiday weekends.

### nova.x7 · Great North Mountain Traverse
The Virginia-West Virginia border ridge past Big Schloss and Half Moon, on trail that sees a fraction of Shenandoah's traffic. Dry camping on sandstone with enormous sunsets.
