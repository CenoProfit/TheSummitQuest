### sierra.w1 · Lower Yosemite Fall
Paved loop to the base of the lower fall. In May the spray soaks the bridge and everyone on it. By September it can be bone dry.

### sierra.w2 · Bridalveil Fall
620 ft, and the first thing you see coming through the tunnel. The wind pushes the base of it sideways most afternoons.

### sierra.w3 · Vernal Fall Footbridge
Paved but relentlessly uphill to the bridge below the fall. Most people stop here, and it is a fair place to stop.

### sierra.w4 · Nevada Fall via Mist Trail
Granite stairs cut beside Vernal Fall, then up again to the top of Nevada. You will get wet in spring, and the stairs are slick when you do.

### sierra.w5 · Wapama Falls
Across the O'Shaughnessy Dam and along the reservoir to footbridges that run under the falls. In high water the bridges close, and for good reason.

### sierra.w6 · Chilnualna Falls
A staircase of cascades above Wawona, climbing 2,400 ft. Quiet, because everyone else is in the valley.

### sierra.w7 · Carlon Falls
A flat walk up the South Fork Tuolumne to a wide swimming pool below the falls. Runs all summer when the big falls have quit.

### sierra.s1 · Mirror Lake
A seasonal pool under the north face of Half Dome. Reflects in spring, is a sandy meadow by August, and is worth it either way.

### sierra.s2 · Cook's Meadow Loop
Boardwalk through the meadow with Half Dome one way and Yosemite Falls the other. The single best half hour in the valley.

### sierra.s3 · Grizzly Giant Loop
Mariposa Grove, and a sequoia with limbs thicker than most trees are tall. Roughly three thousand years old.

### sierra.s4 · Tenaya Lake Shore
A white sand beach at 8,150 ft with granite domes on every side. The water is snowmelt and feels like it.

### sierra.s5 · Lembert Dome
A short climb up glacier-polished granite for the full sweep of Tuolumne Meadows and the Cathedral Range behind it.

### sierra.v1 · Glacier Point
3,200 ft directly above the valley floor, looking straight at Half Dome. The most view per step anywhere in the park.

### sierra.v2 · Taft Point
Fissures split the rim and drop hundreds of feet, with no railing worth the name. Stand back from the edge.

### sierra.v3 · Sentinel Dome
A bald granite dome with a 360 degree view: the crest one way, the Central Valley haze the other.

### sierra.v4 · Tunnel View
El Capitan, Bridalveil and Half Dome in one frame. It is the photograph everyone has already seen, and it still works.

### sierra.v5 · Olmsted Point
Half Dome from behind, which almost nobody sees, across a field of glacial erratics left sitting on bare rock.

### sierra.m1 · Cathedral Lakes
Up through lodgepole to two lakes under Cathedral Peak, which is as close to a perfect mountain as the range offers.

### sierra.m2 · May Lake
A short climb to a lake sitting directly under Mount Hoffmann, near enough the geographic centre of the park.

### sierra.m3 · Gaylor Lakes
Straight up from Tioga Pass at 9,900 ft, so it hurts immediately. Old silver mine ruins above the upper lake.

### sierra.m4 · Elizabeth Lake
A steady climb to a lake in a cirque under Unicorn Peak. Busy trailhead, quiet lake.

### sierra.m5 · Dog Lake
Shallow, warm by Sierra standards, and one of the few lakes up here you might actually swim in.

### sierra.h1 · Upper Yosemite Fall
Sixty switchbacks and 2,700 ft to the top of the tallest fall in North America. Relentless, exposed, and hot by ten in the morning.

### sierra.h2 · Half Dome via the Cables
4,800 ft of climbing and then 400 ft of bare granite up two steel cables. Permit only, and people die on it. Start at four in the morning.

### sierra.h3 · Clouds Rest
Higher than Half Dome and looking down on it, along a summit ridge a few feet wide with a couple of thousand feet either side.

### sierra.h4 · Mount Hoffmann
The geographic centre of the park, and the view Muir sent people to. Loose talus for the last stretch.

### sierra.h5 · North Dome
The only trail that puts you level with the face of Half Dome, looking straight across at it. Passes under Indian Rock arch on the way.

### sierra.h6 · El Capitan Summit
Up the Yosemite Falls trail and out along the rim to stand on top of the biggest granite face on earth. Climbers come up the other way over several days.

### sierra.l1 · Panorama Trail
Glacier Point down past Illilouette Fall to the top of Nevada, then the Mist Trail home. All downhill and it still ruins your knees.

### sierra.l2 · Pohono Trail to Dewey Point
The south rim end to end, with Taft, Dewey and Crocker points hanging over the valley one after another.

### sierra.l3 · Mono Pass
An old trade route over the crest at 10,600 ft, past collapsed miners' cabins still standing in the thin air.

### sierra.l4 · Merced Lake
Up the Merced through Little Yosemite to a long lake in a granite trough. Most people take two days; it goes in one if you start early.

### sierra.x1 · John Muir Trail: Happy Isles to Tuolumne
The first stretch of the JMT, and the one most people never come back from unchanged. Past Nevada Fall, Sunrise, and Cathedral Pass.

### sierra.x2 · Grand Canyon of the Tuolumne
Tuolumne Meadows to Pate Valley down a staircase of cataracts. Waterwheel Falls throws water backwards off the rock.

### sierra.x3 · Vogelsang Loop
The highest of the High Sierra camps at 10,300 ft, over two passes with nothing but rock and sky between them.

### sierra.x4 · Ten Lakes Basin
Over a 9,700 ft saddle and down into a basin holding more lakes than the name promises. Grandstand views on the way in.

### sierra.x5 · Ostrander Lake Overnight
A steady climb through old burn to a lake under Horse Ridge. The stone ski hut on the shore has stood since 1941.

### sierra.x6 · Hetch Hetchy to Rancheria Falls
The valley Muir lost, now under water, walked end to end. Bears here are bolder than anywhere else in the park.

### sierra.x7 · Young Lakes Loop
Three lakes stepped up under Ragged Peak, with the whole Cathedral Range laid out behind you on the way back.
