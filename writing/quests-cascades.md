### cascades.w1 · Sol Duc Falls
Three channels dropping into a mossy slot canyon, crossed by a footbridge that puts you directly over it. Old growth the whole way in, and it rains here about two hundred days a year.

### cascades.w2 · Wallace Falls
Three tiers totalling 265 ft, with the middle viewpoint the one on every postcard. Close enough to Seattle that the lot fills by nine every weekend of the year.

### cascades.w3 · Snoqualmie Falls
268 ft, taller than Niagara, dropping beside a hydroelectric plant that has run since 1899. Sacred ground to the Snoqualmie Tribe and the opening shot of Twin Peaks.

### cascades.w4 · Franklin Falls
A 70 ft fall into an amphitheatre with the I-90 viaduct passing overhead, which sounds terrible and is not. It freezes into a blue wall most winters.

### cascades.w5 · Marymere Falls
A 90 ft ribbon in deep forest at the foot of Mount Storm King, reached on the flat until a short set of steps at the end.

### cascades.s1 · Rattlesnake Ledge
1,160 ft to a rock shelf over Rattlesnake Lake and the Cedar River watershed. The single busiest trail in the state, and the ledge is unrailed with a long drop.

### cascades.s2 · Naches Peak Loop
A loop around a small peak on the Rainier boundary that hands you the mountain over a tarn from the far side. Late July wildflowers here are the best in the state.

### cascades.s3 · Hoh Rain Forest: Hall of Mosses
Twelve to fourteen feet of rain a year, and every branch carrying club moss. Sitka spruce and western hemlock over 250 ft tall. There is nothing else like it in the country.

### cascades.s4 · Lake 22
A cirque lake under the cliffs of Mount Pilchuck, reached through a research forest of old growth cedar with boardwalks over the wet ground.

### cascades.s5 · Deception Pass & Rosario Head
A 1935 steel bridge 180 ft above a tidal strait where the current runs at eight knots. Below it, headland trails through wind-shaped fir out to the water.

### cascades.v1 · Hurricane Ridge
A mile above Port Angeles with the whole Olympic interior across the valley and Vancouver Island behind you. Deer graze the meadow and ignore everybody.

### cascades.v2 · Sunrise, Mount Rainier
At 6,400 ft this is the highest point in the park you can drive to, looking straight up the Emmons Glacier, the largest glacier in the lower 48.

### cascades.v3 · Artist Point, Mount Baker
The end of the road at 5,100 ft with Baker on one side and Shuksan on the other. Mount Baker Ski Area a few miles below holds the world record for snowfall in a season.

### cascades.v4 · Mount St Helens: Windy Ridge
The closest you can drive to the crater, four miles out and looking straight into it across Spirit Lake, which still carries a raft of logs blown down in 1980.

### cascades.m1 · Skyline Trail at Paradise
The signature day on Rainier: 1,700 ft up through the flower meadows to Panorama Point, level with the Nisqually Glacier and looking down onto it.

### cascades.m2 · Tolmie Peak Lookout
A 1930s fire lookout above Eunice Lake with Rainier filling the view behind it. The quietest of the classic Rainier viewpoints because the road in puts people off.

### cascades.m3 · Maple Pass Loop
2,000 ft to a ridge above Lake Ann with the North Cascades stacked in every direction. Larches turn gold in the last week of September and the whole state comes to see it.

### cascades.m4 · Mount Ellinor
A steep Olympic summit with a resident herd of mountain goats that are entirely unbothered by people, and a view over Hood Canal to Rainier.

### cascades.m5 · Summerland
A meadow basin on the east side of Rainier reached along a stretch of the Wonderland Trail, with the Fryingpan Glacier above and marmots everywhere.

### cascades.h1 · Mount Si
3,150 ft in four miles, the training climb every Seattle mountaineer has done a hundred times. The Haystack at the top is a genuine scramble most people skip.

### cascades.h2 · Cascade Pass & Sahale Arm
Switchbacks to a pass used for thousands of years, then a ridge climbing into a glacier basin with peaks on every horizon. The finest alpine walking in the state.

### cascades.h3 · Mount Storm King
2,000 ft in under two miles to a knife edge above Lake Crescent, with fixed ropes on the last stretch and a drop on both sides. Short, and one of the most exposed trails in the state.

### cascades.h4 · Camp Muir
4,700 ft from Paradise to a stone hut at 10,188 ft, the high camp for the summit climb. Above Pebble Creek it is all snowfield and there is no trail at all.

### cascades.h5 · Hidden Lake Lookout
3,300 ft to a 1931 lookout perched on a rock spine at 6,900 ft, with the whole North Cascades laid out around it. The building is open to anybody who reaches it.

### cascades.l1 · Colchuck Lake
2,300 ft to a turquoise lake under Dragontail Peak and the granite wall of Aasgard Pass. It is the gateway to the Enchantments and a full day in its own right.

### cascades.l2 · Mount St Helens: Harry's Ridge
Along the Boundary Trail into the blast zone to a ridge looking straight into the crater and down on Spirit Lake. Named for Harry Truman, the lodge keeper who would not leave the mountain in 1980.

### cascades.l3 · Spray Park
Past Spray Falls into a high meadow basin on Rainier's northwest side that many people rate above Paradise, without the road or the crowd.

### cascades.l4 · Grand Valley from Obstruction Point
A high traverse across open Olympic ridge to a chain of lakes in a glacial valley. The road to the trailhead is the highest in the park and the most alarming.

### cascades.x1 · The Wonderland Trail
All the way round Mount Rainier with 22,000 ft of climbing, crossing every one of its river valleys and glaciers in turn. One of the great long trails in the world.

### cascades.x2 · The Enchantments Through-Hike
Up Aasgard Pass into a granite basin of larches, tarns and mountain goats at 7,800 ft, then down to Snow Lakes. The most sought-after permit in the state.

### cascades.x3 · Olympic Coast: Shi Shi to Rialto
Wilderness beach walking past sea stacks and tide pools, with headland crossings on fixed ropes and sand ladders. The tide decides when you move and when you wait.

### cascades.x4 · Hoh River to Blue Glacier
Seventeen miles up the rainforest valley at almost no grade, then 3,700 ft in the last stretch to the moraine and the ice at the foot of Mount Olympus.

### cascades.x5 · Copper Ridge Loop
A high ridge above the Chilliwack valley in the far north of the park, with a fire lookout on the crest and the Canadian peaks filling the horizon.

### cascades.x6 · Mount St Helens: Loowit Trail
All the way round the volcano across the 1980 blast zone, through pumice desert, boulder fields and canyons cut by lahars, with almost no shade and almost no water.

### cascades.x7 · PCT: Snoqualmie to Stevens Pass
The Alpine Lakes section of the Pacific Crest Trail, ridge after ridge of granite and tarns, and one of the most consistently beautiful stretches of the whole 2,650 miles.

### cascades.w6 · Twin Falls
A 150 ft fall on the South Fork Snoqualmie with an observation deck built out over the gorge, in second growth that has grown back around old stumps ten feet across.

### cascades.w7 · Boulder River Falls
A waterfall that drops straight off a mossy wall into the river opposite the trail, in one of the last stands of low elevation old growth left in the state.

### cascades.w8 · Bridal Veil Falls & Lake Serene
A tiered fall on the way to a lake sitting directly under the north face of Mount Index, which rises 3,000 ft straight out of the water.

### cascades.w9 · Comet Falls
320 ft in a single plume, the tallest easily reached fall in Mount Rainier National Park, with Van Trump Park and its meadows above it.

### cascades.s6 · Discovery Park Loop
534 acres of bluff, forest and beach inside the city limits, with an 1881 lighthouse below and the Olympics across the water.

### cascades.s7 · Little Si
The smaller neighbour of Mount Si with a fraction of the climbing and a rock summit that still looks down the whole Snoqualmie Valley.

### cascades.s8 · Ebey's Landing Bluff Trail
A prairie bluff on Whidbey Island above the Strait of Juan de Fuca, walked out along the top and back along the beach, with the Olympics filling the western horizon.

### cascades.s9 · Nisqually Vista
A paved loop from Paradise to an overlook of the Nisqually Glacier, with the flower meadows on either side and Rainier directly above.

### cascades.v5 · Ruby Beach
Sea stacks standing out of the surf with driftwood logs the size of buses piled against the headland. The most photographed beach on the Washington coast.

### cascades.v6 · Diablo Lake Overlook
A reservoir the colour of antifreeze, from rock flour ground off the glaciers upstream, ringed by the sharpest peaks in the state.

### cascades.v7 · Picture Lake
A small tarn that reflects Mount Shuksan almost perfectly on a still morning. It has a fair claim to being the most photographed mountain scene in America.

### cascades.m6 · Chain Lakes Loop
Round Table Mountain past four alpine lakes with Baker on one side and Shuksan on the other for most of the circuit. Blueberries everywhere in September.

### cascades.m7 · Blue Lake, North Cascades
A gentle climb to a lake under the Liberty Bell spires, with larches turning gold around it at the end of September.

### cascades.m8 · Snow Lake
The largest alpine lake within an hour of Seattle, in a rock bowl under Chair Peak, reached over a saddle with the whole Alpine Lakes Wilderness beyond.

### cascades.m9 · Skyline Divide
Two miles of climbing through forest, then miles of open ridge meadow walking straight at Mount Baker with nothing in the way.

### cascades.h6 · Mount Pilchuck Lookout
A 1921 fire lookout restored and perched on a summit boulder at 5,340 ft, with Rainier, Baker, Glacier Peak and Puget Sound all visible from it.

### cascades.h7 · Granite Mountain
3,800 ft to a lookout above Snoqualmie Pass, one of the steepest sustained climbs on the I-90 corridor and a training standard for Rainier.

### cascades.h8 · Mailbox Peak
4,000 ft to a summit with an actual mailbox on it, stocked with whatever the last person left. The old trail is one of the hardest maintained miles in the state.

### cascades.h9 · Kendall Katwalk
A ledge blasted across a granite face on the Pacific Crest Trail, wide enough to walk and narrow enough to remember, with the Alpine Lakes beyond.

### cascades.l5 · Ptarmigan Ridge
From Artist Point out along a bare volcanic ridge toward Mount Baker, past snowfields that never melt, with the mountain growing the whole way.

### cascades.l6 · Marmot Pass
Up the Big Quilcene through old growth to a 6,000 ft pass looking into the Olympic interior, with wildflowers in July and goats on the ridge.

### cascades.l7 · Goat Lake
A long gentle walk on an old mining road to a lake in a cirque, with Goat Falls dropping in at the far end and the remains of a 1900s townsite along the way.

### cascades.l8 · Royal Basin
Seven miles up the Dungeness to a hanging basin under Mount Deception, with a glacier tarn above it and marmots in every talus field.
