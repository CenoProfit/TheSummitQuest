### sedona.w1 · West Fork of Oak Creek
Thirteen creek crossings between walls that close to fifty feet apart. Wet feet are not optional and the parking lot fills before nine.

### sedona.w2 · Slide Rock State Park
A natural chute worn into the sandstone creek bed, on what was an apple farm until 1985. The orchard is still standing.

### sedona.w3 · Fossil Creek Waterfall
Travertine dams holding back water the colour of antifreeze, in the desert. Permit only from April to October, and the road in is rough.

### sedona.w4 · Wet Beaver Creek: The Crack
Three flat miles of high desert, then a slot in the rock with a deep swimming hole at the bottom and a ledge people jump from.

### sedona.w5 · Parsons Trail, Sycamore Canyon
A spring that turns a dry red canyon green for four miles, then stops. The second largest canyon in the state and almost nobody in it.

### sedona.s1 · Cathedral Rock
Six hundred feet of climbing in half a mile, up a slickrock chute you use your hands for. Short is not the same as easy.

### sedona.s2 · Devils Bridge
The largest sandstone arch in the red rock country, and you can walk out on it. Go at sunrise or queue for the photo.

### sedona.s3 · Bell Rock & Courthouse Butte Loop
Flat all the way round two of the most recognisable buttes in Arizona, with an optional scramble up the side of Bell Rock.

### sedona.s4 · Airport Mesa Loop
A mesa in the middle of town that the whole valley is visible from. The sunset overlook is a hundred yards from the car park and packed.

### sedona.s5 · Sunset Crater Lava Flow
Walking on a lava flow from an eruption around 1085 that the people farming here watched happen. The cone itself is closed to climbing.

### sedona.v1 · Grand Canyon: Mather Point & Rim Trail
Ten miles of paved rim with a shuttle running the length of it. A mile deep, ten miles across, and no photograph has ever worked.

### sedona.v2 · Desert View Watchtower
Mary Colter's 1932 tower at the east end of the park, with Hopi murals inside and the Colorado visible turning south below it.

### sedona.v3 · Merry-Go-Round Rock, Schnebly Hill
Up a rough dirt road onto the rim above Sedona. High clearance strongly advised, and worth every mile of washboard.

### sedona.v4 · Mogollon Rim: Rim Lakes Vista
The edge of the Colorado Plateau: two thousand feet down and two hundred miles long. Zane Grey wrote most of his westerns from below it.

### sedona.m1 · Boynton Canyon
Red walls narrowing to a box canyon with a stand of Douglas fir at the end, four thousand feet lower than fir has any business growing.

### sedona.m2 · Soldier Pass & Seven Sacred Pools
Past the Devils Kitchen sinkhole, which is still collapsing, to a line of potholes in the rock and a cave above them.

### sedona.m3 · Bright Angel to Three-Mile Resthouse
Twenty one hundred feet below the rim, with water in summer and a roof. Going down is optional. Coming back up is not.

### sedona.m4 · Lockett Meadow & the Inner Basin
Inside the collapsed caldera of the volcano, through the largest aspen stand in the Southwest. The first week of October is the reason to come.

### sedona.m5 · Kendrick Peak
A 10,418 ft volcano standing alone west of the Peaks, with a 1912 lookout cabin on top and no crowd on any day of the year.

### sedona.h1 · Humphreys Peak
12,633 ft, the highest ground in Arizona, with the last mile above treeline on a saddle where the wind does not stop. Storms build by noon.

### sedona.h2 · South Kaibab to Skeleton Point
The first sight of the Colorado from the trail, two thousand feet down a ridge with no water and no shade on any part of it.

### sedona.h3 · Wilson Mountain
Twenty three hundred feet above Sedona to the highest ground overlooking the red rocks, through a burn and out onto an open plateau.

### sedona.h4 · Bear Mountain
Nineteen hundred feet in two and a half miles over four false summits, on ledges and slickrock. The hardest short day in Sedona.

### sedona.h5 · Mount Elden Lookout
Twenty four hundred feet straight out of Flagstaff on switchbacks through the 1977 burn, to a lookout still staffed in fire season.

### sedona.l1 · Bright Angel to Plateau Point
Out to a shelf above the Inner Gorge with the river twelve hundred feet below. Three thousand feet back up, and the Park Service asks you not to try it between May and September.

### sedona.l2 · Loy Canyon to Secret Mountain
Up the back of the red rocks onto a mesa nobody has ever built a road to. The last mile to the rim gains a thousand feet.

### sedona.l3 · Sycamore Rim Trail
Around the head of Sycamore Canyon past two waterfalls, a sinkhole lake and the grade of a logging railroad that closed in 1930.

### sedona.l4 · Kachina Trail
Along the flank of the Peaks at nine thousand feet through aspen and old lava, contouring rather than climbing the whole way.

### sedona.x1 · Havasupai: Havasu, Mooney & Beaver Falls
Ten miles in to blue-green water on the Havasupai reservation, with no day hiking allowed. The permit lottery opens in February and sells out in minutes.

### sedona.x2 · Grand Canyon Rim to Rim
South Kaibab down, North Kaibab up, a vertical mile each way and four billion years of rock in between. The North Rim closes for winter in October.

### sedona.x3 · Bright Angel to Phantom Ranch
To the only lodging below the rim, built in 1922 and still supplied by mule. Beds are a lottery a year out; the campground is easier.

### sedona.x4 · Sycamore Canyon Traverse
Twenty one miles of canyon with no maintained tread for long stretches and water only where the spring runs. Wilderness since 1935.

### sedona.x5 · Arizona Trail: Mormon Lake to Flagstaff
Through the ponderosa at seven thousand feet on the stretch of the eight hundred mile Arizona Trail that ends in town.

### sedona.x6 · The Highline Trail, Mogollon Rim
Cut in the 1870s to link the ranches under the Rim, and it still runs the whole way. Two thousand feet of escarpment above you for four days.

### sedona.x7 · Weatherford Trail & the Inner Basin
A 1920s carriage road that never opened, contouring to the saddle at 11,800 ft. Snow lies in the basin into June.
