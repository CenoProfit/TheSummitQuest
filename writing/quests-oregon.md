### oregon.w1 · Multnomah Falls
620 ft in two tiers with a 1914 footbridge across the middle of it. The most visited natural site in the state and the reason the Gorge has a traffic problem.

### oregon.w2 · Punch Bowl Falls, Eagle Creek
A trail blasted into the basalt in 1915 with cable handlines along the cliff edge, leading to a bowl-shaped fall in a green amphitheatre.

### oregon.w3 · Silver Falls: Trail of Ten Falls
Ten waterfalls on one loop and you walk behind four of them. The largest state park in Oregon and, by common agreement, the best day of waterfall hiking in the country.

### oregon.w4 · Toketee Falls
120 ft dropping through a slot in a wall of columnar basalt into a deep blue pool. It is on the licence plates for a reason.

### oregon.w5 · Tamanawas Falls
A 100 ft curtain on the east side of Hood that you can walk behind, reached along Cold Spring Creek through old growth.

### oregon.s1 · Mirror Lake
A small lake that hands you the reflection of Mount Hood on a calm morning. Everybody in Portland has this photograph.

### oregon.s2 · Angel's Rest
A rocky prow 1,500 ft above the Columbia with the river running east and west below you. The burn from 2017 opened the view and it has not grown back.

### oregon.s3 · Cannon Beach & Ecola Point
Haystack Rock standing 235 ft out of the sand with tide pools at its base, and a headland above with a view down the whole coast.

### oregon.s4 · Misery Ridge, Smith Rock
A brutal little climb up a tuff wall to a ridge above Monkey Face, a 350 ft spire, with the Crooked River looping below and the Cascades on the horizon.

### oregon.s5 · Cape Perpetua: Thor's Well
A collapsed sea cave that fills and drains with each swell, alongside the Spouting Horn and Devils Churn on the same stretch of shore.

### oregon.v1 · Crater Lake: Rim Village
1,943 ft deep, the deepest lake in the country, sitting in the caldera of a volcano that removed itself about 7,700 years ago. The blue does not photograph.

### oregon.v2 · Vista House, Crown Point
A 1918 stone rotunda on a basalt promontory 700 ft above the Columbia, built as a rest stop on the first scenic highway in America.

### oregon.v3 · Timberline Lodge
A WPA lodge built by hand in 1937 at 5,960 ft on Hood's south side, and the only place in North America with year-round lift-served skiing.

### oregon.v4 · Painted Hills
Bands of red, gold and black laid down over 35 million years of changing climate, each colour a different ancient soil. Best in late afternoon or after rain.

### oregon.m1 · Green Lakes
Three lakes in a basin between South Sister and Broken Top, walked in past Fall Creek falls and a wall of obsidian from a 2,000 year old flow.

### oregon.m2 · Tom Dick and Harry Ridge
Above Mirror Lake to a rocky ridge looking straight across at Hood with Jefferson and the Sisters lined up behind it.

### oregon.m3 · Garfield Peak, Crater Lake
1,000 ft above Rim Village to a summit at 8,060 ft looking down the whole caldera with Wizard Island directly below.

### oregon.m4 · Tam McArthur Rim
A 1,200 ft climb to the lip of a lava plateau, with Broken Top and the Sisters filling one side and Three Creek Lake far below on the other.

### oregon.m5 · Dog Mountain
2,800 ft straight up the Washington side of the Gorge to a bald covered in balsamroot in May, with the river laid out below.

### oregon.h1 · South Sister
10,358 ft, the third highest in Oregon and the highest anyone can walk up without a rope. A crater lake sits in the summit and the last thousand feet are loose red cinder.

### oregon.h2 · Cooper Spur
The highest a walker can get on Mount Hood, 8,500 ft on a cinder ridge beside the Eliot Glacier with the crevasses right there beside the path.

### oregon.h3 · Munra Point
An unmaintained scramble to a knife-edge basalt fin above the Gorge. Steep, loose, exposed, and among the most consistently underestimated hikes in the state.

### oregon.h4 · Mount McLoughlin
9,495 ft, the highest point in southern Oregon, with Shasta on the horizon and Klamath Lake spread out below.

### oregon.h5 · Saddle Mountain
1,600 ft to the highest point in the Coast Range, with wire mesh bolted to the steepest rock and, on a clear day, the ocean on one side and Hood on the other.

### oregon.l1 · Eagle Creek to Tunnel Falls
Six miles up the creek to a 165 ft fall with the trail blasted straight through the rock behind it, cable in the wall to hold on to.

### oregon.l2 · Cleetwood Cove & the Boat
The only legal way down to the water at Crater Lake: 700 ft of switchbacks to the shore, and the boat out to Wizard Island from the dock.

### oregon.l3 · Broken Top & No Name Lake
An eroded volcano with a milky turquoise tarn sitting in the crater under a hanging glacier. One of the great secret places in the Cascades, and no longer secret.

### oregon.l4 · Oregon Coast Trail: Cape Lookout
Two and a half miles out along a headland that runs straight into the Pacific, with a 400 ft drop on both sides and grey whales passing below in season.

### oregon.x1 · The Timberline Trail
All the way round Mount Hood at treeline, crossing every glacial river on the mountain. Built by the CCC in the 1930s and still the finest loop in Oregon.

### oregon.x2 · Three Sisters Loop
Round all three Sisters through obsidian flows, lava fields and lake basins, with the Pacific Crest Trail forming the western half.

### oregon.x3 · Crater Lake Rim Loop
The Pacific Crest Trail's alternate route round the caldera rim, with the lake below you almost the whole way and no water anywhere on it.

### oregon.x4 · Oregon Coast Trail: Tillamook Section
Beach walking, headland climbs and estuary crossings between Cape Meares and Neskowin, on a trail that runs the entire 380 mile coast.

### oregon.x5 · Eagle Creek to Wahtum Lake
The whole length of Eagle Creek past Punch Bowl and Tunnel Falls to a lake on the Pacific Crest Trail, then out over Indian Mountain.

### oregon.x6 · Rogue River Trail
Forty miles down a wild and scenic river canyon past mining cabins and rapids, walkable in spring and autumn when the high country is buried.

### oregon.x7 · PCT: Santiam to Timberline
Past Three Fingered Jack and round Mount Jefferson through the Jefferson Park meadows, the most photographed alpine basin in the state.
